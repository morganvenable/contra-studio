# Figure readings

Where ContraDB text is ambiguous, the standard reading the engine uses, one
line each, for review.

## Formations and progressions

- "Improper or proper", "improper/proper", "all improper", misspellings and qualifiers ("imroper", "improper - waltz tempo", "duple minor"): improper duple minor.
- "Progressed improper", "improper progressed", "begins progressed", "improper, but with 1's below 2's": the improper start; the first figures meet whoever the couples face.
- "Beckett", "becketa", "Becket (no matter)", "Becket L", "duple Becket": clockwise Becket.
- "Duple Becket (reverse progression)", "Becket ccwkoi", "Beckett ccw": counterclockwise Becket.
- A Becket dance labeled one way round whose own figures carry the couples the other way ("Becket CCW" with "slide left along set"): it goes the way its figures go, when it then works out completely.
- "Indecent Becket", "Becket cw (gents on right, ladies on left)": Becket with each lark on the partner's right; neighbors are still the dancer across.
- "... double progression", "dbl prog": the same formation; the figures carry each couple two places a round, so the corpus check expects two places.
- "Triplet", "proper triplet": three couples proper (larks on the larks' side), 1s at the top; couples are renamed 1s, 2s, 3s by place at the end of each time through, and the check expects the 1s at the bottom.
- "Longways", "longways set, 4-6 couples", "four couple longways set": a proper whole set, renamed by place each time through; the check expects the top couple at the bottom.
- "Sicilian circle": couples face couples round a circle, partners side by side, lark on the left; the 1s face one way round and the 2s the other, and the figures pass each couple on to the next couple coming; the 1s and 2s never change direction.
- "Four face four", "four facing four": lines of four facing along the hall (two couples side by side, each lark on its partner's left); each couple's minor set is the couple straight across; lines pass on to the next line, and a line at an end waits and each couple turns round in place.
- "Circle mixer", "mixer": one ring facing in, the robin on her partner's right; at the progression each lark's partner becomes the robin then on his right (usually the one just swung).
- A circle mixer passes each lark on the same number of robins every time through, so whether everyone partners everyone depends on the ring size ("Lucky Seven" brings six couples back to their own partners): the check asks it of a ring of 5 couples.
- "Circle": the same ring, for dances that keep partners.
- "Long lines forward & back" in four face four and Sicilian circles: the lines (or couples) facing each other walk forward and back.
- "Promenade" in a ring: counterclockwise (lark inside) unless the caller says otherwise; with no distance named, as many couples' places as an easy walk covers.
- "Square dance", "square break", "four couple square": four couples in a square, couple 1 with its back to the band, numbered counterclockwise; heads are couples 1 and 3; corners stand beside each other in neighboring couples; partners are found where they stand at the end of each time through.
- "Scatter mixer": drawn as one ring (a circle mixer); scattering and finding a new partner happen round the ring.
- "One couple", "singlet", "solo/couple" (Zoom contras): drawn in an improper set, the imagined neighbors danced by real couples.
- A row that names who dances ("star right 4 places (heads)", "middle 4 star right", "ones and twos circle left", "hands six"): the figure for just those dancers; everyone else stands.
- "Stand still", claps and stomps, "heel, toe": everyone keeps their place.
- "Triple minor", "triple proper", "triad", "dutribecket": drawn as one minor set of three couples, proper; the 1s progress to second place each time through and couples are renamed by place; the step on to the next minor set down a long set is not drawn.
- "Sawtooth cw", "quadruple minor Becket", "Becket quadruplet", "Becket men in center ...": drawn as clockwise Becket.
- "Cross contra", "long waves, larks facing out", "proper, ladles traded": drawn from the plain improper (or proper) start; rows that need the traded start stay labeled.
- "Reverse progression" (improper, indecent): the same formation; where the couples travel is left to the figures, and the check expects the usual places.
- "Contra mixer", "duple mixer", "proper mixer", "Becket mixer": drawn as the plain contra formation; the partner change is not drawn.
- "Quadruple minor", "grid square", "square with ninepin": drawn as a square of four couples. "Five couple horseshoe": drawn as a ring of five couples.
- "Three face three" (and spellings): lines of three facing lines of three round a circle, drawn as a set with no ends like a Sicilian circle; each trio's center dances the lark's part and the ends the robins'; neighbors (opposites) are the dancers straight across.
- Not built, because their dancers do not come in couples or the set has a one-off shape: "5 dancers", "line of 5 people", "9-person mixer", "three-person mixer", "circle of threes", "tempest".
- "Proper" (duple minor): every lark on the larks' side, partners facing each other across the set at the start (lines facing).
- A pair figure that names a place ("ones allemande right 1½", "heads do si do"): those dancers dance it with their partners.
- A dance whose rows carry two progression marks progresses twice a time through (a double progression), whatever its formation line says.
- In a ring, a row that says the figure makes a new partner ("this neighbor is your new partner", "that's your new partner") is the progression, even without the progression sign.
- A role moving "one place" or to the "next partner" in a ring goes one couple (two dancer places) round; "move to your next partner" with no role named means the larks.
- A robin's new partner "behind her" after a counterclockwise promenade is the lark on her left; a lark going to the "next" robin goes to the robin on his left (his corner), as in the Circassian Circle.
- "Turn over the left/right shoulder & go to new partner": walk into the middle, turn that way, come out two places over on that side, and end facing in.
- "Slide left along set" in a ring: everyone faces in and slides round the ring, one place per four beats.
- Grand right and left: each change is one dancer place; a right hand passes right shoulders with the lark inside; "count to N" pulls by N-1 and meets the Nth beside them, facing in.
- "Sashay in & out": partners face with two hands, slide about 1 m into the middle and back, and end on their places.
- "Partners swing right elbow", "elbow swing": an allemande by that hand.
- "Neighbors scatter promenade" in a ring: promenade with the robin now on your right, who becomes your partner.
- In a ring, partners after the progression are found as if each lark faced the center: the robin beside him on his right.
- A heads or sides figure for two couples (star, circle, right and left through, chain, promenade across ...) is danced in the middle of the square: the couples step in to a minor set, dance it, and step back out to the nearest places they came from.
- "First corners X", "neighbors X (corners)": the corner (the dancer beside you in the next couple); "next neighbors X (next corner)": the robin now on the lark's left, by place.
- "Swing the one you meet", "3rd neighbors swing (that other lark)": the robin now in the lark's partner place.
- A plain grand right and left (right and left grand, weave the ring) in a square goes until partners meet, halfway round.
- Promenade home: counterclockwise to the lark's home place; a couple already home goes once all the way round.
- A grand chain crosses to the opposite couple (half a star) and ends with a courtesy turn beside the one who receives them.
- "Heads separate, go around two": pass behind the side couple and end in lark-lark-robin-robin lines facing across; in a line roll away the lark rolls the robin.
- "Same four": the dancers the row before named.
- A cast with no place named goes one place; the couples it passes move one place toward where the casting couple started.
- "Up the center and cast to the bottom": up the middle between the couples above, round the top of the set, down the outside.
- "Sashay/lead down and back" with no place named goes as far as a steady walk (0.75 m a beat) carries it, then back; "down & up & down" goes bottom, top, bottom.
- "Promenade to the bottom" in a proper whole set is led side by side on own sides with inside hands.
- A whole-set circle or oval with no amount goes as far as 0.75 m a beat carries it, a whole number of places; a circle of a whole set goes round a true ring of places.
- A star with no places named is the whole group, once round (six places in a triplet).
- A two-hand turn goes clockwise, once round; "swing two hand" is drawn as a two-hand turn.
- Contra corners (16 beats, split 3+4+2+4+3): partner by the right, first corner by the left, partner right, second corner left, partner right back to place; the first corner is the one on the active's left after crossing (Chorus Jig).
- Peel the banana / cast and arch: the others follow their leader; "top couple cast off, make an arch at the bottom" means the others follow and come up through the arch, as in the Virginia Reel.
- Strip the willow: both actives strip at once; partners turn right 1½ at the top, once between, ½ at the bottom; each turned couple moves up one place.
- In four face four and Sicilian circles, each group of four is a Becket minor set turned a quarter: couples face across within the group, so a swing ends facing the other couple and a pass through goes on to the next.
- In four face four, "circle left/right 4 places" (or 8) with no ring named is the ring of eight from the two lines (4 places is halfway, the lines trade places); other places are danced in rings of four. A ring of eight all facing out stays facing out.
- Arch and dive: the arching couple raises joined inside hands and the other couple ducks under; "(back up)" backs through to the places just left, the named couple arching.
- "As couples do si do": two couples holding inside hands do-si-do as units, passing right sides.
- "Neighbor/partner right/left elbow": an allemande by that hand, once round unless an amount is given.
- "Swing, end facing the next / new lines / direction of travel" in these formations ends on the pair's own side facing out of the minor set.
- In four face four, "opposite" is the dancer you face (the neighbor); "balance the ring of 8" is a ring balance in rings of four.
- In a whole set (triplet, longways set), a partner swing from across the set ends back in the partners' own places, facing each other across.
- A whole set renamed by place (triplet, longways set) renames once, at the end of each time through, even where several rows carry the progression mark.
- "Top four" and "bottom four" are the four standing nearest the top or the bottom now, by place.
- Becket progression where partners do not face the same way (out of a star, or a ring balance and California twirl): the new neighbors are the couple standing square to them one place away that they did not just dance with; couples pair only when they choose each other.
- In a big ring, a circle given in times round goes as far as a steady walk covers in its beats, in whole places (8 beats is about halfway).
- Going "to a new partner" through the center ends facing the new partner, after one and a half turns over the named shoulder.
- A roll away in a ring: the rolled dancer passes in front, spinning once, to the roller's place; both end facing in.
- Footnote markers "[n]" and glosses "(corners)", "(former partner)" are notes, not figures.
- In a triplet, a neighbor is the dancer of the other role in the other couple the row names (lark 1 with robin 2, robin 1 with lark 2).
- In three face three, a trio's "partners" are the rest of its own trio; the basket swing turns about once every 4 beats, a whole number of times, and ends in the same line.
- An elbow turn is a hand turn by that hand; a whole turn ends back in the line facing the line's way.
- A swing with no side line to end on (same roles, facing lines of three) ends where it began.
- "Neighbors do si do 1½ as throuples": every neighbor pair at once, the lines keeping hands; the 1½ is the progression.
- "X WHILE / Y as well": both rows dance at once.
- "Same as B1" repeats B1's calls, without B1's progression mark unless the row has its own.
- Hey for three: a figure eight whose loops end on the end places, 0.5 m either side of the line, dancers a third of the loop apart; in a half hey the ends trade places.
- Dip and dive: when two couples meet and neither has arched, the couple facing up arches; couples then alternate; a couple at an end facing out turns as a couple; "until everyone is back home" is twice the number of couples.
- In a whole set, "right left through at the top" is the 1s and 2s across.
- A cloverleaf turn single turns toward the named dancer and ends facing along the set, away from the middle of the minor set.
- "Ones cross": partners pass right shoulders and face in on the other side, in the same place.
- Set to corners: a step toward the corner and back, ending facing each other; corners as in contra corners.
- "Four couple longways set": a proper whole set of exactly four couples.
- In four face four, "corners swing" squares the set: corners swing and open out without trading places onto a square of eight; the middle couples become the heads, the end couples the sides; "partners swing" from the square swings back into lines.
- In four face four, corners are the neighbors round the ring of eight on the far side from your partner: the middle two of a line, and an end dancer with the dancer facing them.
- Line cast-off: the left-hand lark of each line leads it single file round the end, halfway, to the other line's place, everyone facing the way their line faced.
- "Lines of four, pass through and back" (Dead Simple): the lines pass through to the next line (the progression mark is on that row).
- A quick (two-beat) arch and dive keeps the arch a little open and the divers a little wider so nobody has to run.
- "Other four": the other four from the row before (heads and sides, larks and robins swap).
- "One from each couple forward and back": the robins.
- A star of one role (or of the same/other four) walks into a small star (0.62 m) and back out to the places it reaches; a bare "star right/left" by four named dancers goes once round.
- Texas Star: "larks to the center for a right hand star" goes ½ round and stays in; "star left/right, pass your partner" goes ¾ round to the place beside the next robin; in the star promenade the inside dancer is 0.62 m out and the partner 0.7 m beyond on the same spoke; "butterfly whirl to put X in the center" turns each couple half round about its middle.
- "Monkey in the middle" ("at random" made deterministic): the monkey is the lark nearest the band; every lark swings his corner, the monkey's pair first, then the pair across, then all four; corners become partners.
- Pass through and cross trail: one walk to the point opposite across the square, passing right shoulders, the robin crossing in front of the lark; they end facing out.
- In "separate and go around", each dancer goes away from their partner.
- In a square, partners standing across the square (or in the middle) meet at the nearest two free places before a swing, balance or do si do.
- A brisk promenade home draws the square in toward the middle on the way.
- "5 dancers": a ring of five places 1.53 m apart; the dancer on the first's left becomes first each time through.
- Solo strip the willow: the active turns each other dancer once round, left hand first, going round the ring to the left from just inside it.
- Birdie in the cage: the ring closes up, circles once round and opens back to its places; "slide back into place" backs the birdie out to the empty place.
- "Line of 5": numbered 1-5 from stage left; renumbered by place each time through (no.1 goes to the far end).
- Double dolphin hey: a hey for three on a figure eight, tandems 1.0 m nose to tail; thread the needle: 2 and 3 arch, no.1 leads the rest once through and back to place.
- "Circle of threes", "three-person mixer": lines of three in a big ring facing counterclockwise, the inside end on the center's left; "promenade anywhere" is drawn round the ring; a basket swing "with a different person in the center" stops a third of a turn on.
- "9-person mixer": a Becket set of four couples plus a lonesome fifth lark at the top facing down; "swing at random": each robin swings the lark on her other side round the loop, the leftover lark becomes the lonesome dancer.
- "Tempest": a line of four 1s facing down, then the 2s, one couple per side line facing in; down the hall the line walks 2 m and each couple wheels to face up while the 2s slide up one level.
- Couples out at the ends of a Becket set dance with ghosts: they repeat the matching couple of the nearest minor set (crossing the set when the active couples do), and a couple that comes out at an end changes ends by walking half way round its ghost minor set over the next eight beats or more. Figures with shadows or on a diagonal leave them be.
- A row marked as the progression that comes before the couples have passed (the mark on the figure that starts it): the new neighbors are found at the end of the first later figure where they have passed, up to the next mark. A mark on the first row that does not work there means the round starts progressed: the couples meet new neighbors at the end of the round.
- A Becket dance whose source marks no progression (or only its last row) progresses where its rows meet the new neighbors: at the end of a row that goes "to new neighbors", or just before the first row danced "with next neighbors". Improper dances keep the end of the round. Failing that, "circle left 3 places, pass through" is its progression.

## Heys, stars, petronella, roll away, Rory O'More, passes

- A trailing note that only says whom the dancers meet next or dance with ("to new neighbors", "with shadows", "past neighbors", "(no clap)", "a little more to face partners", footnote marks) is drawn as the figure it names; a note that adds motion ("larks turn back over right shoulder", "end in long waves") leaves the row undrawn.
- Ricochet ("robins ricochet"): the two who would pass in the middle walk in, loop back like the dancers at the ends of the hey, and go back out to their own side; the rest of the hey is unchanged.
- A ricochet with no time named is the named dancers' first meeting in the middle ("robins ricochet to start" is that first meeting too).
- "Until partners meet the second time": the hey goes pass after pass and stops, slowing down, as those two come face to face; it does not finish that pass.
- "Larks drop out" at the end of a star is a note (they let go where the star leaves them).
- "Star left 3/4" is a star of 3 places; "1/2" and "halfway" are 2.
- "Balance & petronella left" moves everyone one place to the left, turning to the left.
- Roll away from a ring ("balance the ring, larks roll away partners"): the rolled dancer crosses in front of the roller, toward the middle, and both end facing the middle.
- "Pull by right" with no one named, or named as shadows: the dancers who face each other pull by.
- "Balance & larks Rory O'More right": only the pairs holding that hand with a lark slide; anyone else stands still.
- Revolving door ("robins take right hands and drop off partners on other side"): the door dancers turn once around by that hand, back to their places; the dancers they carry go halfway and are left on the other side, facing in.
- Cross trails: the first named pair crosses in the first half of the beats, the second pair passes in the second half.
- Arch and dive ("twos arch ones dive"): the two couples trade places straight through each other, the divers drawn close under the arching couple's raised inside hands, and end facing the way they walked (on to the next couple).
- Half figure eight ("ones half figure 8 below"): the robin goes through the middle first unless the row names a leader; each dancer loops behind and around the outside of the standing dancer on the far side and ends in the partner's place, facing across; the other couple stands still.
- A hey "until larks meet" with no time named stops at their first meeting.
- Roll away with shadows: the shadow is the nearest dancer of the other role who is neither partner nor neighbor.
- Zig zag ("zig left zag right"): the two pairs of the minor set (partners unless neighbors are named) trade places as units, stepping to the named side as they pass, everyone keeping their facing (the stand-in for a half poussette).
- A hey on a diagonal runs along the line between the two diagonal couples, as an ordinary hey turned onto that line.
- Star promenade "larks pick up partner" / "scooping your partner" is the ordinary star promenade; an allemande "to pick up partner for a star promenade" is the allemande alone.
- "Balance the star" is drawn as a ring balance (step in toward the middle and back).
- Gate ("larks gate neighbors to face into the set"): each gater pivots in place holding the near hand while the other walks around them, the shorter way round (forward when equal), until both face the named way, arm's length apart; "(cw)"/"(ccw)" notes are not drawn separately.
- "Balance & petronella turning to face partners", "(standard 4 person petronella)", "roll away ... half sashay alone set" (along) and "star ... & look for new neighbor" are read as the plain figure.
- Becket progression when partners stand across the set (after "circle left 3 places" then a zig zag or half poussette): each couple joins the couple beside it along the set that it was not just dancing with; neighbors are the other-role dancers of the two couples.
- A roll away in under four beats: the rolled dancer's spin is reported as styled motion (like the Rory twirl), since a full turn in two beats is faster than an ordinary walk turn.
- Contra corners: the actives turn by the right ¾ (4 beats), each turns the other-role corner at their end of the set by the left once around (4¾), the actives turn by the right ½ (2½), and each turns the other corner by the left once around (4¾), ending back to back in the middle; corners come from the neighboring minor sets too, and step in to arm's length and back.
- Mad ricochet (Don Flaherty): a mad robin whose dancers in front walk all the way into the middle, meet the same role from the other side with both hands, and bounce back; the other role does the same on the way back.
- Star thru: lark's right hand with robin's left, the robin turns a quarter left under the arch and the lark a quarter right; they trade places and end side by side facing the lark's right. "Neighbors California twirl (star through)" from facing is drawn as a star thru.
- Dolphin hey: a hey for three along the set on one figure-eight track, the named couple dancing as one (one close behind the other) and the two others a third of the track apart; the track is turned so the first pass is the one the row names; a full one ends everyone at home. Extra passes ("one extra crossing", "one more time") are separate rows or are left undrawn.
- A short motion after a figure ("; robins turn back over right shoulder", "robins turn around", "larks turn out over left shoulder", "and pass thru to new neighbors") is drawn as its own call over the row's last two beats (a quarter of a shorter row); turning back with no shoulder named goes over the right.
- Facing star ("facing star clockwise 3 places with robins putting their left hands in and backing up"): the named role (the robins when none is named) faces against the way the star turns and backs up, their other hand in the middle.
- Petronella "2 places" / "petronella-nella" / "x2": two places with a turn for each; "1½ places" ends on a diamond with two turns, facing in.
- Star notes read as the plain star: "larks drop out (on home side)", "robins falling in / stepping in behind partners", "a bit more until ...", "just halfway", "with shadow #1", "seek your new neighbor", "to take right hands with next neighbors".
- A trailing turn with no one named ("robins balance & pull by right, turn back in") is done by the dancers the row named; a turn named alongside a pass ("larks pass by ..., robins turn over right shoulders") is drawn right after the pass.
- Hey rows: "pass partner right to start" / "(pull by r)" restate the first pass; "passing by the left" restates the shoulder; blank shoulders ("____ in center") take the row's "passing ... shoulders", else rights; "until partners meet back on home side" is the usual "until partners meet"; "when the robins meet the second time they ricochet" is a ricochet on that meeting; "larks finish an ordinary hey" is a note.
- Zig zag rows saying whom the pairs pass or meet, or who leads out ("larks lead out, robins in to face new couple", "into a ring with new neighbors") read as the plain zig zag; one forming a wave or another figure does not.
- "Ones full figure 8": two half figure eights, around one standing dancer then the other, ending home.
- "Pull by right diagonal" with no diagonal side named: the diagonal on the side of the hand; whoever faces across that diagonal pulls by.
- Contra corners notes "home file", "opposite file", "ending progressed", "(alternate with twos)" read as plain contra corners.
- A petronella over more than one place cuts inside the ring and its extra spins are reported as styled motion (like the Rory twirl).
- Circular hey: around the ring of places like a grand right and left without hands, straight from place to place, the named pair passing first by the named shoulder and the passes alternating; full is four changes, half two.
- Star promenade "larks pick up neighbor": the neighbor walks the outside instead of the partner.
- A pass by too long for its beats (larks crossing the minor set diagonally in two beats) goes as far toward the other's place as a walk allows, veering most where the two cross; dancers turn to face the way they walk more slowly when the turn is big.
- "A while B" rows run B during the last part of A, on other dancers (a meanwhile figure); a row whose two parts move the same dancer stays undrawn. Named Rory slides join no new hands, since the wave may be re-formed by the others meanwhile.
- Stars by eighths ("star l 7/8", "hands across star l 9/8"): four dancers go n/8 of the way round, n/2 places; half places end on a diamond.
- "Star ____ - wrist grip - 4 places": a star of whole turns ends where it began whichever hand is in; drawn as a left-hand star.
- Chevron ("larks chevron into neighbors' place"): neighbors trade places; the named role walks a V in toward the middle (passing the other of their role by the right), the others walk straight across a little to the outside.
- Flaherty's "1/2 hey, robins start right shoulders, upon facing neighbor veer to right to face new neighbor": the half hey; the veer to the next neighbor is left to the next row.
- "Star left 3 places and take both hands with your partner", "balance & petronella to a left hand star": the hands are the next figure's to take.
- Rory O'More in long waves (after "form long waves" or a long-wave balance): each pair standing side by side along a side line, facing opposite ways with the slide hand toward each other, slides past; dancers with no one on that side (the ends) stay.
- A zero-beat "form long waves - larks face in, robins face out" before "balance & Rory O'More": that balance forms the long waves, the named role facing in.
- A hey "to long waves, larks facing out" (or "finish in long waves", "end in long lines"): the hey ends as usual, in the corners facing across; the wave balance after it forms the long waves, the role named facing in (or the other role, when the row names who faces out).
- A gyre after a figure that leaves hands held: the gyring dancers let go as they start. Two gyres side by side (after lines forward) go round tighter so the pairs keep clear.
- "Three-quarters hey": six passes, ending at the next meeting; "five changes of a hey": five passes (or until the meeting the row names).
- Short hey wordings ("1/2 hey, larks start right shoulder", "half hey, larks start passing left, partners pass right, robins pass left", "hey, partners passing left shoulders to start"): a role starts in the middle by that shoulder, partners or neighbors start on the ends by it; the passes spelled out after are a reminder. A hey with no size is full in 12 beats or more, half otherwise.
- "Until again facing neighbor": until the neighbors meet the second time; "until partners meet on the side of the set they started": the second meeting in a hey of 12 beats or more.
- A zig zag of pairs standing side by side across the way they travel (after "circle right 3 places"): the pairs step far enough to the side that their near dancers pass clear.
- Colonnade ("ones arch, colonnade!"): four trades of a quarter of the beats each, the two of one role by the right shoulder (the one not in the arch going under it), then the other role, then each again; the role named first ("robins trade") goes first, else the larks; "left colonnade" by the left. The arch itself is not drawn.
- "Down the hall, ones turn as a couple, twos turn alone" (or "centers (ones) turn as a couple while ends turn alone", "larks wheel, robins turn alone"): the two named, next to each other in the line, wheel to the left as a couple; the others turn alone toward them.
- A butterfly whirl from the middle of the set (after a star promenade along the set): the couple ends on the side the other couple is not heading to, or else on the side its clockwise whirl reaches first.
- Mad robin with a direction in the row ("larks in front (clockwise)"): the role in front decides the turn; a direction that disagrees is read as seen from the dancers' side. "Ones in front" (one of the ones on each side line): each side line turns so its one goes in front, a mirrored mad robin when the two turn opposite ways.
- Half poussette "pull back then left/right": the pairs go round the middle of the minor set the way that keeps them in it; a left or right that disagrees is read as seen from the other dancer.
- "Partners Nevada twirl (robins twirl the larks to swap & face next neighbors)": a California twirl. "Robins turn out" (after a star): the robins turn alone.
- "Ones split the twos, then cast home" / "return to place": the ones walk between the twos (who step apart and back), a little past, then separate and come back up the outside to their places, facing as they began.
- Rory O'More in long waves goes on past the next dancer, never back past the one just slid past; at an end with no one to slide past, a dancer stays.
- "Down the hall and turn as a couple, come back, bend line to circle" in 16 beats: down the hall and turn (half the beats), then up the hall and bend into a ring.
- Gyre, roll away, petronella, Rory O'More, slice, twirl and butterfly whirl rows with a remark after the figure ("partners gyre once, then face neighbors across", "larks roll away neighbors (corner I)", "balance & petronella (doesn't matter where it takes you)", "slice left (long lines slice)"): the figure as usual; a remark that adds motion leaves the row undrawn.

## Relationships, hand turns, do-si-dos, swings, balances, waves, lines

- Shadow: your nearest dancer of the other role who is neither your partner nor your current neighbor, mutually and within 2.7 m; "1st"/"2nd shadow" is whichever is nearest at that moment. With one minor set there is no shadow, so nobody moves.
- Previous neighbors ("prev neighbors"): the neighbors before the last progression when they are within reach; before the first progression, the nearest other-role dancer outside your minor set.
- Same roles: the other dancer of your role in your minor set (both larks and both robins dance at once).
- A note after a figure that names no further figure ("allemande right 1½ to partner", "do si do once back to neighbor", "swing, face across") is dropped: the figure's own ending stands.
- "Allemande left once or twice" and similar alternatives take the first choice.
- Orbit: the orbiters walk around the minor set's center at their own distance, facing the way they walk, ending as they started relative to the center.
- Dance in to a long wave in the center: the role steps straight in from its side line, facing the way it walked; the two of each minor set stand 1.2 m apart level with the minor set's middle, so hands join within a minor set and between minor sets only where they reach.
- "X dance out while Y dance in": X back out to the side they came from, still facing in.
- A long-wave balance row of 4 beats spends 2 beats forming and 2 balancing (the same split as a short ocean-wave row).
- Two-hand turn: clockwise unless named, facing each other throughout, letting go and stepping back to the starting distance after a whole or half turn.
- Couples do-si-do ("do si do as couples/as a couple", either relationship named): each couple dances as one dancer around the couple it faces, passing right shoulders, partners closing to 0.8 m apart in the middle.
- Stand still keeps every hand that is held.
- "Down the hall"/"up the hall" with no ending: the lines just arrive, facing the way they walked; "up the hall backward" walks up still facing down.
- "Long lines back" alone backs the lines out to the side lines, facing across.
- "Ones/twos in the center" after down the hall is left to the line the dancers form.
- Jersey twirl is drawn as a California twirl; "all California twirl" is partners.
- "Robins turn alone over their right shoulder": only the robins turn, clockwise seen from above.
- "Circle right 4 places and turn alone", "pass through and turn alone": the last 2 beats turn alone.
- A swing ending "in a line of four facing down" (or up) is the usual swing ending; the down/up the hall that follows forms the line.
- Walk forward (to the next wave/neighbors): dancers facing along the set walk one place; dancers facing across walk out to their side line; whoever arrives side by side facing the other way within 1.2 m takes hands.
- A zero-beat "turn alone" after a figure of 6 beats or more takes that figure's last 2 beats.
- A zero-beat "form an ocean wave" on the last row forms the wave during the first balance of the next time through; a dance that opens by balancing the wave its last row forms starts in that wave.
- A zero-beat "form a long wave in the center" says where the dancers already are and adds no motion.
- Do-si-do 1¼ (or ¾, 1¾) stops a quarter on, side by side facing opposite ways, 0.9 m apart, ready for a short wave; no hands until a figure takes them.
- A swing whose pair straddles the middle, one dancer in the very center and the other on a side line, ends on that side line.
- Couples do-si-do: partners not quite facing the same way square up to the direction between them over the first beat.
- "Balance right and left", "balance forward and back", "balance the star", "balance in a clover", "balance long wave(s)": a plain balance of what the dancers hold now; dancers holding hands in a line that is neither a minor-set wave nor a ring balance right and left in place.
- "Balance the ring and twirl one place to the right" is a balance and petronella; "balance the diamond" is a ring balance.
- "X allemande right ½, Y pass left in the center (swing through)": the allemande takes the first half of the beats, the pass the second.
- "Swing, slide left (to progress)" in Becket: the swing ends 2 beats early and the couples slide left along the set.
- "Allemande ... to pick up partner for a star promenade": the star promenade that follows picks up the partner.
- "Larks turn alone" (one role named): only that role turns, toward their partner. A zero-beat "turn alone" after a short figure takes the first 2 beats of the next figure of 6 beats or more.
- "Balance the ring" with a trailing note that names no other figure ("[current neighbors]", "with partner") is a ring balance; "balance left, then right" with a pair is a pair balance.
- "As couples do si do" with no amount is once around.
- Ones down the center: the couple walks one place down between the other couple, 0.6 m apart with inside hands; down the outsides runs 0.6 m outside the side lines; up the center/outsides walks one place back up.
- Partners swinging out of a wave down the middle of the set (both on the center line) end on the side the lark faces.
- "Start in long waves, ... X face in" (the notes before the figures): when the dance opens with a wave balance, that balance forms the long waves on the side lines; "start in short waves, right hand to neighbors" forms a short wave with robins in the middle by the left unless the notes name the middles.
- "Allemande ... to long wave(s)/forming long waves": the pair keeps hold and stays close instead of stepping back out, ending facing across on a side line; the role named facing in is not checked.
- "Do si do 1¼ to a (short) wave": the minor set takes wave hands as the do-si-do ends.
- Partners straddling the middle evenly when they swing ("larks take neighbors" from a ring) swing on the lark's side.
- "(Swing through)" written beside an allemande ½ and a pass in the center only names that pair of figures; "X pass by left shoulders (swing thru)" is the pass alone.
- "Neighbors balance right" is a right-hand balance; "long lines balance" is long lines forward and back.
- "Ones turn alone": only the 1s turn, toward their partners.
- "Circle left 3 places, partners swing" in one row: half the beats each.
- "Down the hall forward then backward": half the beats down, half back up still facing down.
- A zero-beat "form an ocean wave" right before an allemande ("swing through") adds no motion: the allemande takes hands where the dancers are.
- "X allemande ... hold on to form waves / to a short wave / to a wave of four" (X a role): a short wave with X in the middle.
- "... and take hands with next neighbors in long lines", "take hands": the long lines or ring that follows takes the hands.
- "Long lines forward & back, X roll away partners/neighbors (on the way back)": half the beats forward, then the roll away back to the side lines; "roll away the robins" is partners.
- "Pass through, walk forward (to new wave)": half the beats each.
- "Balance (the wave) and move straight ahead to a new wave": a 4-beat balance, then walk forward; "dance forward to form new waves and balance": walk forward, then a 4-beat balance.
- "Swing, end facing across to rejoin ring / end in a ring / form a ring": the ordinary swing ending; the ring that follows forms itself.
- A jersey twirl with no one named is partners.
- Allemande path: the pair closes straight in over the first part of the turn and (a whole or half turn) steps straight back out over the last part, turning at 0.38 m from the joined hands in between; it walks the whole path at one steady pace. Quarter turns still end at the wave spacing (0.45 m).
- A couple still crossing over at an end when down the hall starts stays out of the hall; waiting couples cross passing close by each other (the half turn squashed to 0.4 of a circle along the set), so two couples waiting side by side do not meet.
- A swing drifts to where it ends over the first 70% of the figure (not the whole of it), so pairs swinging near the middle clear each other.
- A twirl turns the dancers to face each other over at least 0.75 beat, longer the farther they turn (half a beat per radian).
- "X ... to a wave of four / ocean wave / short wavy line" after any figure (circle, do-si-do, twirl, pass through, swing): the figure takes all but 2 beats, then the minor set steps into the wave the row describes, robins in the middle by the left and neighbors by the right unless named; "to a wave and balance" leaves 4 beats to form and balance.
- "X allemande ... while Y shift left/right" or "... while Y walk forward / move straight ahead": the others slide one place (1.8 m) to their left or right, or walk one place ahead, keeping their facing.
- "Balance the ring, larks trade places / robins trade right shoulder": a ring balance, then that role passes by the right shoulder to trade.
- "Balance yourself": everyone balances right and left alone in place.
- "Partners balance and twirl to swap inside hands": a right-hand balance and box the gnat.
- "Balance the ring and roll partner/neighbor ..." with no roller named: the larks roll.
- "Neighbors two-hand turn counterclockwise 1/2/3+ times": once around.
- "Larks give & take partners and swing": half the beats each.
- "Ones split the twos with a handy hand allemande", "ones mirror allemande neighbors", "mirror do si do, ones splitting twos": each pair turns (or passes) the way that sends the 1s through the middle of the set first, so the two sides mirror each other.
- "Balance (and move) straight (ahead) to a new wave": balance, then walk forward; "balance new wave of four ..." is a wave balance.
- "Balance the ring & pass through": ring balance, then the pass through; "and walk forward" after a circle takes its last 2 beats.
- Petronella "to long waves, larks face out": the petronella; the long waves are left to what follows.
- Forming an ocean wave when the hands named do not fit how the dancers stand: dancers facing someone across pass through by the right shoulder into the wave (stopping level, half a wave gap apart); failing that, each middle takes the other end dancer of its minor set (the one it can reach). Seen in Mange Tak (1014): after the neighbor swing everyone faces their partner across, so the wave that forms holds partners on the ends.
- A wave balance where the dancers hold long waves (not a wave of four in each minor set) balances side to side in place, holding on.
- "X allemande ... to a short wave" (X a role): the other two of the minor set step into the line the turning pair ends on, each beside the middle they hold (by the right to partner or neighbor when the row says so, otherwise the nearer end), facing opposite that middle.
- "... to a diamond (X in the center)": the figure takes all but 2 beats, then the four shift an eighth of the way around their ring so that X ends on the middle line of the set (clockwise when no one is named), facing in.
- Cloverleaf: the four of a minor set join hands in a ring facing out; "cloverleaf left 3 places" turns it three quarters, left being clockwise seen from above as for a circle left. "Bend into a cloverleaf" bends the lines into a ring; the cloverleaf figure turns it out.
- "Robins trade passing right in the middle", "... by the left shoulder": the robins pass by that shoulder and trade places.
- "Balance and move straight ahead to a new wave" in a 4-beat row: too short to balance as well, so the dancers only walk forward.
- "Balance the ring, shift one place to the left": a ring balance, then circle left one place.
- Dancers stepping into an ocean wave whose straight paths would pass closer than 0.75 m bow apart sideways, each to its own side.


## Circles, chains, passes and shifts

- A row with a trailing note ("dance starts with this couple", "to new couple", "with original neighbors", footnote marks, "briskly", "smush") is drawn as the figure it names; the note is not drawn.
- "The first time through just circle 3/4" and similar first-time variations: every time through is drawn the usual way.
- "X or Y" (an alternative the caller may choose): X, the first one named, is drawn.
- A row naming two figures ("circle left 2 places and roll neighbor across") is split: a circle takes two beats a place (at most what leaves the second figure two beats, at least what leaves it no more than eight); a pass through, slide or turn alone at the end takes two beats; otherwise the row is halved.
- "Single file" circles are drawn as the ring path with no hands, dancers turned to walk along the ring and back in to face the center at the end.
- "Circle left 1 place (single file promenade)" is a single-file circle one place.
- "Roll neighbor across / along (the set)" inside a ring: the larks roll unless another role is named; whether the roll goes across or along is read from where the pair stands, and a direction the source names is checked against it.
- "Left-hand chain" (and "chain, pull by left"): the mirror image of a chain: pull by left hands, then a right-hand courtesy turn with the turner on the right, wheeling clockwise.
- "Chain over and back", "full chain: across and back": two chains, half the beats each.
- "Chain back (to partners)", "chain over", "chain across (the set)", "chain straight across": an ordinary chain.
- "Chain to shadow / trail buddy / shadow 1": an ordinary chain; the turner is whoever stands on the far side.
- "Right left through" with "straight across", "with neighbors/partners", "w/o hands", "use a twirl instead of courtesy turn", "you pull past your partner": an ordinary right and left through (hands and courtesy turn drawn).
- "Along the set right left through": an ordinary right and left through with the couples facing along the set.
- Square through two, three or four: the pulls alternate between the named pairs ("four" repeats the first two), a balance starts each pair of pulls when the row says "balance & pull by", each balance takes 4 beats and the pulls share the rest evenly. Words after the last pull ("and go to new neighbors", "face in") are notes.
- "Mad robin ½": half way around, the two on each side line trade places; "mad robin twice": two times around. Notes after "in front" ("around neighbors", "eyes on partner") are not drawn (a "mad ricochet" is a figure of its own, not read as a mad robin); a direction (cw/ccw) is checked.
- Mad robins travel at a steady walk with short ramps at each end (not a C2-eased orbit), so a 6-beat mad robin stays at walking speed.
- "Slide right along set past neighbor / with neighbor", "shift left with neighbor", "slide left to new couple": an ordinary slide along the set, one place.
- "Slice left and straight back in long lines / to (face) new couple": an ordinary slice.
- "Box circulate - X cross while Y loop right" with no balance: the box circulate alone, from waves already formed.
- "Pass through up and down": a pass through along the set.
- "Pass partner (by right) along set": a pass through along the set by the right shoulder (or the shoulder named).
- "X give & take partners, X take partners / Y bring back Z": an ordinary give & take; the second half restates the first.
- "Entire set oval left/right", "big oval circle left/right": the whole set joins one ring (along each side line and across each end) and moves one place every two beats; hands are drawn only where they reach (dancers on a side line stand 1.8 m apart).
- "Butterfly whirl moving right (along the line) to next neighbors", "butterfly whirl (shift left to next couple)": the whirl, then a 2-beat slide one place that way.
- "Half poussette - ... then left clockwise / (current neighbors) / around next neighbors": an ordinary half poussette; the words after the turn are notes.
- "Pass through with partner / past partners / passing partner; going to shadow": an ordinary pass through.
- Gyre notes ("gypsy", "look into partner's eyes", "walk around partner") are not drawn; a gyre may go a quarter at a time (1¼).
- "Partners promenade, shifting right along the side": the promenade, then a 2-beat slide right.
- Turning in to face (circles, gyres) takes longer the farther a dancer has to turn: at least one beat, at most half the figure, at a walking turn rate.
- Circle hands join only where they reach, so a dancer still arriving from the far side of the set takes hands as they close in.
- "Slice left and diagonal back": the couples slice out on the diagonal and back the same way, ending home.
- "Right and left through across": an ordinary right and left through.
- "Ladles chain left diagonal": the chain on the left diagonal; a row ending "to" after a reminder ("chain (to new neighbor)") is the figure alone.
- Becket (minor-set) dances progress at their slide or slice along the set, or at their figure on a diagonal: when the source marks only the last row as the progression (or none) and the dance has exactly one such figure, the new neighbors are met there.
- In Becket lines a couple, for finding new neighbors and diagonals, is a lark and the robin standing on his right on his side line facing the same way (after a diagonal chain that is not his partner); otherwise his partner.
- Box circulate "loop right/left" names the way the loopers turn (over the right or left shoulder); they always travel to the crosser's place on their own side line, even when that is the other way.
- A slide or slice along the set starts from any facing on the side lines (out of a ring, a star, or waiting at an end): dancers turn to face across as they set off, taking longer the farther they turn.
- "Balance & box circulate - ..." with words after the loop ("ladles cross set, men rotate right", "with shadows"): an ordinary balance and box circulate.
- "Butterfly whirl to face in / across": an ordinary butterfly whirl (it ends facing in).
- "Promenade neighbor (to new couple)": neighbors promenade across.
- "Pass through with/past shadows", "chain (to) shadow": an ordinary pass through or chain. A star "with shadows" is not read this way (it names other dancers) and stays undrawn.
- Square through with no balances may go as fast as a pull by a beat (three pull bys in 4 beats); the physical check judges it.
- A row a pattern half-reads ("ladles chain to partner (the dance starts with same couple)") gets the same note dropping as any other row.
- A circle shorter than four beats closes up less and keeps a steadier pace; closing up always takes at least 0.8 beat.
- A gyre between dancers far apart (opposite corners) walks in, around and out along one path at a steady pace, going around close enough (never under 0.27 m from the middle) to stay at a walk.
- A right and left through straight from a line of four (after up or down the hall): the line bends as the couples set off, ends forward and middles back. A long crossing (to a diagonal couple) takes up to 65% of the beats before the courtesy turn.
- Figures on a diagonal (circle, chain, right and left through): the two couples close up along their lines (at a steady pace) to stand straight across from each other over the first beats (up to 4, half the figure), and step back out to the places on their sides over the last ones.
- In Becket lines, couples on a side line face across whichever way each dancer is turned (finding new neighbors after a ring); a lark's couple is the robin on his right, else the robin on his left.
- Left right through: the mirror image of a right and left through (pull by left hands, courtesy turn the other way round).
- "Ladles slide right to partner", "meanwhile ladles slide left to meet partner": only that role slides one place to its own right or left, letting go of hands.
- A zero-beat "slide left along set" row takes the last 2 beats of the figure before it (6 beats or more), or the first 2 of the figure after it when it opens the dance.
- J-hook, "promenade and loop (left/wide/large)", "promenade and hook", "promenade, shift ccw around set": the promenade, then the couples go on one place along the set in 2 beats; the way is the one the row names ("to the right"), otherwise the formation's progression (left in Becket clockwise and elsewhere, right in Becket counterclockwise).
- "Neighbors (partners) promenade along the set on the right/left": the whole set walks half way round the oval (right = counterclockwise), single file, facing the way it goes, no hands; a second such row brings everyone home.
- "Circle left 3 places to (a) wave of four": the circle, then the last 2 beats open into the wave; unnamed, robins take left hands in the middle and neighbors right hands on the ends.
- Promenades shorter than 8 beats spend 68% of the beats crossing and leave more of the wheel for opening out; partners close in on the pair's middle steadily as it walks.
- A butterfly whirl takes hands only once partners are within reach, and each turns to the couple's facing over a beat or more.
- After a figure on a diagonal that leaves couples straight across from new couples (a diagonal chain or right and left through), those foursomes are the minor sets for the figures that follow; relationships change only at the progression.
- ContraDB's plain "Becket" does not say which way round: clockwise is the usual reading, and a dance whose own slides take the couples counterclockwise is checked and stored as Becket counterclockwise when that is how it works out.

## Where two builders read the same figure (integration)

- Contra corners: in a triple-minor group or triplet, the ECD reading above (back to place); in a duple minor contra, the contra reading above (corners in the next minor sets, actives end in the middle back to back).
- Arch and dive: rows both readings parse ("ones arch twos dive") use the rolling-arches reading; the arch-and-dive-couples reading takes the wordings only it reads ("twos arch, ones dive through", trailing notes).
- "Balance & pull by" and "balance & petronella" in 6 beats: 4 beats balance, 2 beats pull by or petronella; under 6 beats the two share the row evenly.
- A pass by more than twice as long as a walk covers in its beats (across a big ring, to the next dancer) goes all the way, reported for speed, instead of stopping short.
- A star promenade steps in over 2.4 beats, at most 60% of the figure: a 4-beat half star promenade steps in gently, a full 8-beat one settles sooner.
- Couples do-si-do and two-hand turn: the contra figures first; where they cannot start, the ones written for other formations.
- "Walk forward" with no one named, in a contra set: everyone walks on one place (to the next neighbors or wave); in lines, rings and squares, forward without coming back.
- "Stand still": hands held stay held; claps and stomps let go.
- "Shadows pass by / pull by": whoever faces each other passes.
- A ring of more than 8 dancers (a circle mixer's whole ring) may stand up to 0.32 m per dancer from its center, so neighbors stay about 2 m apart.
- "Promenade along the set" and other rows both the formation table and the circulation table read: the formation reading (ring promenade, promenade home in rings and squares); the circulation table (the whole-set oval) takes the rest.
- A minor-set dance progresses with couples read as partners; a Becket dance that only works out with couples read as they stand (a robin on the lark's right) is stored that way.
- A pass by that carries the progression walks all the way to the other's place.
- "Ones balance", "twos do si do" in a contra set: the figure dances those dancers itself first; failing that, those dancers with their partners.
- A row whose trailing note adds motion ("robins shift left to meet partners", "to form cloverleaf", "and square the set") stays undrawn rather than drawn without it.

## Hands, lines, chains and partner figures (round 2)

- An allemande whose hand still holds a wave or line (three or more dancers holding hands in a line) lets go of it and turns; a hand held in a lone pair (a courtesy turn, a couple's hand) is still busy.
- "End (the swing) facing (on) the left/right diagonal", "face next neighbors on the slight left diagonal", "look on the left diagonal for ...": the figure ends as usual; the diagonal figure that follows gathers the couples.
- "Swing ..., end swing facing down/up the hall": the usual swing ending; the hall figure that follows turns the lines.
- "X swing (new)", "(the one you met in the chain)", "(diagonal neighbor)", "(the larks who cross over swing their partners)": the plain swing.
- A swing or do-si-do that ends "in a ring", "in a line of four" or "and form a ring": the plain figure; the ring or line forms itself.
- Chain notes ("this is shadow", "after larks drop", "to N", "to the same neighbor they just swung", "- join hands four", "to reclaim partner", "to form a circle with a new couple", "can make it an open chain ...", "there are no roles in a solo dance"): the plain chain.
- Right and left through notes ("with a courtesy turn", "end facing across", "end in a ring", "pull past partner", "identify next neighbors", "conga line ...", "partner swing if no one is there", "power courtesy turn to face down", "both cross and pull their left shoulder back to wheel around", "find new neighbors across ..."): the plain right and left through; "on the 1/2 right diagonal" and "with neighbor on the half-left diagonal" put it on that diagonal.
- "Allemande ... to trade", "(they will be closer than you think)": the plain allemande; "..., X shift left to meet partners" is the allemande while X shift left.
- Pull by notes ("- same dancer", "(swing through)", "2 along the set"); "pass by right shoulders and take right hands as they pass" is a pull by; "trade the wave - neighbors pass by right shoulders across" is neighbors passing by.
- Pass through notes ("improper here", "partner right", "neighbor right", "along to shadows", "straight ahead to new neighbors", "to face sides", "to face a new neighbor", "past partners to face shadows", "and form a ring", "pass your partner by the right to find your shadow"): the plain pass through.
- "Balance wave 4", "balance ocean wave o 4", "balance the wave right & back", "balance left & right wave of four", "balance short waves to the right and left": a wave balance, right then left.
- "Balance the ring, balance in and out", "balance ring", "balance in and out", "balance the star with N1": a ring balance.
- "Balance & Rory O'More right (in long waves)": the balance and Rory O'More.
- "Do si do once as a couple ...", "do si do ¾, as couples" (with "ready to zig", "around neighbors", "to face neighbors"): couples do-si-do; "new neighbors do-si-do", "do-si-do current neighbor": once around.
- Star notes ("next couple", "with shadow 1 & new neighbors", "- reach right hand to next neighbors", "about 3.5 places for larks one", "then with next neighbors", "turn away from the star toward your shadow", "larks are behind partners", "robins stepping in behind their partner", "robins begin, larks join behind partner", "once around and come back to original couple", "robins drop out at the end", "larks drop out at home, robins switch to an allemande grip", "robins joining behind and ending across from neighbors", "(do not rush this)"): the plain star.
- Star promenade notes ("scoop your partner (plunder)", "robins pick up your partner with arm above ...", "partners are scooped up and brought across set"): the plain star promenade.
- "Neighbors balance inside hands / with inside hand(s) / outside hands / with joined hand / with handy hand", "partners balance together & apart / towards each other and away", "balance to and from X": a pair balance toward each other and back, no hands drawn.
- "Partners balance right hands along": a pair balance by that hand.
- "Balance (everyone)" and "same roles balance, robins balance (in the center of the long wave)": a balance of what the dancers hold now.
- Open chain ("robins chain (open chain: robins pull by R, N allemande L 3/4)"): the pull by (40% of the beats, 2 to 4), then neighbors allemande left ¾ instead of the courtesy turn.
- "Do si do ½ to trade places": a pass by right shoulders into each other's place.
- An allemande by a fraction of a turn ("3/8", "5/8", "5/4"): that share of a whole turn.
- A zero-beat row followed by a "MEANWHILE ..." row: both dance at once, taking the second row's beats.
- A zero-beat row in parentheses ("(neighbors pull by left)"): a note on the row before; no motion of its own.
- A zero-beat pass through or pull by: takes the last 2 beats of the figure before it (or the first 2 of the one after) when that figure has 6 beats or more.
- "X take Y" of 4 beats or more with no swing with Y after it: a give and take.
- A pair figure named by partners or neighbors when some pairs stand in different foursomes (after a figure on a diagonal regrouped the set): every pair dances together for that figure, when all are within reach (2.7 m); the foursomes come back afterward.
- A star steps in over at least 1.2 beats (and at an easy pace from a big ring), and a dancer who must turn far to face the way round turns over longer; a star of a big ring (a circle mixer's) stays wide enough that dancers side by side round it are 0.6 m apart.
- An allemande turns each dancer to face the way round at about 2 radians a beat at most (sooner when there is time), and the hands join once both have turned most of the way.
- A figure on a diagonal when only one minor set dances (three couples, or couples out at both ends): nobody has a couple on the diagonal, so everyone stands still for it.
- A balance given other than four beats: two or three beats balance quickly; five or six balance in four and stand the rest; seven or eight balance twice.
- "X two-hand turn, once (around)", "two-hand turn once" (partners), "partners two-hand turn and open to face across", "... any amount, end facing down in line of four": a two-hand turn once; "two-hand turn 3 times": that many turns.
- "Larks cross by the right": the larks pass by right shoulders.
- "Pass through across the set, face out / to meet on the other side", "pass through to partners", "pass through, ones split the twos": the plain pass through.
- "Star ... to (a) long wave(s)": the star; the long-wave balance that follows forms the waves. "Robins dance in to a long wave in the center - balance the wave, and remain", "robins to the center to a long wavy line, balance, and stay there": the long wave in the center and its balance.
- "Larks chain by left hand", "larks chain (larks pull by L, right courtesy turn)": a left-hand chain.
- "Balance (wave of 4), robins allemande left halfway (to face partner / back to neighbor)": a balance of what the dancers hold, then the allemande in the rest of the row.
- "Facing star clockwise 3/4": a facing star of 3 places; "larks star L halfway": a star of the larks, 2 places.
- A zero-beat row that only takes, catches, joins or drops hands ("larks catch left hands", "neighbors join inside hands", "open up to face new neighbors, hands four"): no motion; the figures around it take the hands they need.
- A courtesy turn on its own ("partners courtesy turn", "neighbor courtesy turn", "new neighbor courtesy turn, face across", "robins give partner left hand for a courtesy turn"): the lark turns; he receives the robin where he stands and the pair wheels counterclockwise to face in across the set, the robin on his right a place along (a couple already side by side on a side line keeps its two places). The wheel runs from the way he faces round to facing in: half a turn when he faces out, a whole turn when he already faces in.
- A swing whose pair starts far apart (the two ends of a line of four) closes in at an easy walk (about 0.65 m a beat) before turning, and the swing hold is taken only once they have closed in.
- "Balance the ring, neighbors swing to a wave of four ...", "balance wave of 4, robins allemande left once": a 4-beat balance, then the rest of the row as its own figure.
- "Do si do 1¼ to right hand ocean wave, larks in center", "allemande left 1½ to give right hand to neighbor to make wave of 4", "balance the ring and twirl to wave of four ...": the figure into that wave (the do-si-do, allemande or petronella to a wave of four).
- Forming an ocean wave when the pairs the row names ("neighbors by right on the sides") stand side by side on opposite side lines facing across (after a swing): the wave is built from the row's text, not from who faces whom. It runs through the middle of the minor set along the set; each end keeps its facing and its own half (1.35 m out), its middle steps in beside it (0.45 m out) facing the other way, the middles passing each other on the way. Mange Tak (1014) and similar dances now follow the row. Mange Tak now reaches the end of the round, but the progression cannot rebind there: the dancers end standing in short waves after "allemande ¾ and look for next neighbors". That is the progression rule's case.
- First and second corners in a minor set of numbered couples: first corners are the lark of the 1s and the robin of the 2s; second corners the robin of the 1s and the lark of the 2s; in a square both name the corner. "Corner allemande L 3/2": the corner, left, 1½.
- "Robins courtesy turn larks": the robins turn the larks beside them (neighbors unless partners are named). "Partners courtesy turn 1¼ to face the center": the wheel goes once round more than the turn to face the center.
- "New/next neighbors ..." on a row before the row the source marks as the progression: the stored neighbors when they can dance it, otherwise the next neighbors as the dancers stand.
- Right and left through "(larks on right)", "(robins courtesy turn this same neighbor larks)": the robins courtesy turn (they take the left place).
- One-off rows, each read the standard way (parse-hands.ts round 2b):
  - "Balance and swing", "dosido and swing neighbor": the neighbors.
  - "Swing partner", "all swing": partners swing. "Elbow swing": partners allemande right once.
  - "Right shoulder and melt into swing": a meltdown swing.
  - Swing notes ("end swing, end facing down", "join hands four", "take hands in long lines", "balance optional", "this is a traveling swing", "twirl to the swing on other side"): the plain swing.
  - "Partners balance & swing by balance the ring first": a ring balance, then partners swing.
  - Mirror do-si-dos and handy-hand allemandes "(ones split the twos)": the split readings already in the table.
  - "Larks allemande ____ 1½": left hand.
  - Allemande notes ("keep hands", "scooping up your partner", "while robins drop out", "while robins face counterclockwise", "to trade back to the proper side"): the plain allemande. "While larks loop back over their right shoulders": the larks turn alone meanwhile.
  - Chain notes ("to opposite neighbor", "to corner I", "courtesy turn ¾ ...", "courtesy turn is optional", "then turn"): the plain chain. "Across and back": over and back.
  - Star notes ("and scatter single", "into a new wave", "back", "until larks are on their home side"): the plain star.
  - "Star promenade left ½ and move right along set to new couple": the star promenade, then slide right.
  - Balance spellings ("short wave", "the line right then left", "the wave l, r", "long lines", "lines of three"): a balance of what the dancers hold.
  - "Balance towards and away (neighbor), spin or slide past neighbor": a neighbor balance, then neighbors pass by right shoulders.
  - Give and take and take notes ("to the robins's side", "draw back partner", "with underarm turn"): the plain figure. "Robins give & take partners, swing": then partners swing.
  - "Robins pull partner to your home side into a swing": partners swing.
  - Right and left through notes ("with a second new neighbor ...", "straight across, face partner up & down", "courtesy turn 3/4 til ..."): the plain right and left through.
- Rows that add motion, read the standard way (round 2c):
  - "Balance wave, larks move straight ahead while robins cross left to partner (courtesy turn ...)": a balance, then the larks walk forward while the robins slide left.
  - "Balance wave, robins move straight ahead while larks stay at home": a balance, then that role walks forward.
  - "Balance wave and move on the right diagonal to a new wave": a balance, then everyone moves straight ahead to the new wave.
  - "Robins allemande right 1½ on right diagonal, larks turn to face partner / move up to meet partner": the diagonal allemande.
  - "Robins allemande right 1½, larks cross straight across": the larks pass through meanwhile. "... larks loop right / just turn around": they turn alone meanwhile. "Larks slide R slightly / shift left slightly / slide along set": they shift one place.
  - "Allemande ¾ as if to form a long wave in center": to a long wave.
  - "Allemande shadow left once, allemande partner 1/2": both, in turn.
  - "Robins do si do once while larks go straight across set / larks orbit half": the larks pass through, or orbit clockwise ½, meanwhile.
  - Do-si-do notes about ending in long or short wavy lines: the plain do-si-do; the next figure forms the line.
  - "Star left 3 places ... then larks turn back over right shoulder": the star, then the larks turn alone.
  - "Larks pull by left, robins cast over right shoulder": the pull by while the robins turn alone.
  - "Pass through left shoulders across the set": a pass through by the left.
  - "Larks walk across the set while robins step left": the larks pass through while the robins shift left.
  - "(Balance &) trade the wave - partners passing right ...": partners pass by right shoulders.
- Round 2d readings:
  - Rows that name the long or short wave a swing, do-si-do, star or pass ends in ("partners swing to long ocean wave ...", "robins do si do once to a long wave ...", "star right 4 places, larks push off to end in short waves ...", "robins pass by left shoulders to form long wave ..."): the figure; the wave balance that follows forms the waves.
  - "Same roles balance long wave, larks back out robins come in": a balance, then the larks dance out while the robins dance in to a long wave in the center.
  - "Balance larks turn left half to face partner": a balance, then the larks turn alone.
  - "Larks allemande right once a round to form a new wave of four ...": the allemande to a wave of four. "Neighbors allemande right once and however long to form short ocean waves": 1¼ to a wave of four.
  - "Robins keep left hands to allemande left 1½ meanwhile the larks ... loop over their own right shoulder", "... while larks turn over left shoulder and cast off": the allemande while the larks turn alone.
  - "Robins roll away partners on the diagonal / diagonally so larks trade places": a roll away with a half sashay.
  - Star notes ("larks dorp out at home", "7/8 til larks face next larks", "and walk along the set to find the next couple", "larks face up & down, robins on sides"): the plain star. "Right hands across 3 places, finish in long waves": a hands-across star right, 3 places.
  - "Walk forward to new short/ocean waves and 1/4 more", "all walk forward, on very slight right diagonal": walk forward.
- A half allemande in two beats or less between dancers standing well apart takes the shorter path: it turns closer in (0.27 m from the hands, the pair 0.54 m apart) at an even pace with short ramps, so no one walks faster than a walk.
