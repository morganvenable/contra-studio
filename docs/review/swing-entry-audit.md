# Swing-entry timing audit

The public compilers reject a bare opening swing until an explicit initial-frame and physical repeat-return contract is available. This prevents a source's opening sixteen-count swing from silently spending more than two counts gathering from a distant starting formation. No currently reviewed source begins with a bare swing; ContraDB 881 remains recognized but unplayable under this guard.

A transition need not snap into a rigid, fully held pose at its exact notation boundary. The regression distinguishes already-prepared swings from a short connecting movement whose contact acquisition is shown truthfully. No existing do-si-do route was changed by this checkpoint.

## Durable whole-library regression

`src/swing-entry-regression.test.ts` reads every retained `REVIEWED_SOURCE_IDS` snapshot through the public source reader and compiler. Three- and six-couple complete 2N periods include alternating active/waiting ends and repeat boundaries. Its latest run checked 7,440 active pair entries:

- 7,008 prepared starts have the full joined/back/support frame at the declared boundary and turn during the first count.
- 360 entries after a do-si-do connect inward during 1.12 counts of an eight-count swing. Meaningful orbit begins around 1.17–1.18; the full frame is acquired by 1.6. These are the existing routes in 1972, 671, 1942, 1270 and 3335. A visible runtime warning reports the allocation. The regression bounds this specific short connection rather than allowing its duration to grow proportionally with a longer swing.
- 72 short-wave partner entries rotate immediately while their horizontal frame closes over 1.5 counts. This is the reviewed Undercurrent composition; its two preceding half-turns retain their exact two-count endpoints.

Every entry verifies the source-named relationship, actual three-edge frame, full-strength deadline and relative-pair angular motion. Connecting entries additionally prove inward root movement and the absence of a falsely full frame at the start. Classification uses typed figure and predecessor semantics, never a title or record-ID exception.

This timing test complements, rather than replaces, whole-source identity/finite-period tests and actual articulated-body checks. A change to a transition's timing or geometry still requires source-count, continuity, gait and body review. The five existing do-si-do connections remain candidates for visual-flow refinement; the diagnostic alone does not assert that an instantaneous frame would be better.
